Happened during the BIT-44.6 user check, under track BIT-44 ("Deep research before scope: bit:analyze + bit:ruler").

Scope (BIT-44) says, in its Decisions: "**`research_write` takes a track, a topic, and a body, and overwrites.** Rewriting a topic replaces its file, and there's no delete. ... The first write creates `.bit/research/<track>/`, the way `feedback_add` creates `.bit/feedback/`." Nothing in the scope or its bars says what happens to a track's research folder when the track is deleted or completed.

Bar BIT-44.6's User verifies says: "In `claude --plugin-dir ./bit`, create a throwaway track, run `/bit:analyze` on it, then `/bit:scope` to refine it. ... Then `bp task delete` the throwaway track."

What happened (as relayed by the coordinator, who ran the check in fresh `claude -p --plugin-dir ./bit` sessions):
- A throwaway track, BIT-45, was created, and `/bit:analyze BIT-45` wrote 3 topics to `.bit/research/BIT-45/`.
- `/bit:scope refine BIT-45` called `research_read`, read `index`, `json-shape` and `task-list-cmd`, and wrote a body that cites topic names. The check passed.
- `bp task delete` was run on BIT-45. It moved the track to `.bit/archive/tasks/BIT-45.md` and left `.bit/research/BIT-45/` in place. The coordinator removed the research folder by hand. The coordinator's words: "`bp task delete` leaves the track's `.bit/research/<track>/` folder behind, so I removed it by hand."
- The user asked for this to be recorded and not fixed.

The work required: after deleting a track, its `.bit/research/<track>/` folder stays on disk until someone removes it by hand.
