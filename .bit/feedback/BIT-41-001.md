Happened during bit_scope work on BIT-41 ("Versioning for bp and the bit
plugin"), no bar yet — this surfaced before bit_plan.

Scope (BIT-41) orders four verses with the spike third:

> - [ ] Verse 1 — Cut a version without typing one: `just release <level>` bumps
>   plugin.json, commits, and creates the local tag, and `bp version` reports
>   `1.0.0` instead of a git sha. […]
> - [ ] Verse 2 — Publish a release deliberately: `just release-push` sends the
>   tag to origin as a separate, guarded step […]
> - [ ] Verse 3 (spike) — Settle what "update" actually means end to end: push
>   1.1.0 and observe whether an already-installed consumer picks it up on its
>   own, only on command, or not at all […]
> - [ ] Verse 4 — Know you are behind without going looking: running bare `bp`
>   prints a notice when a newer version is available, shaped by whatever Verse
>   3 found […]

Its Risks entry records the ordering and the reason:

> **Downstream:** Verse 4 depends entirely — automatic updates make the notice
> nearly pointless, manual updates make it obligatory and it must name the
> command to run. Verses 1 and 2 do not depend on the answer.
> **Artifact:** kept — the pushed 1.1.0 is this track's real release, not a
> throwaway.
> **De-risk before planning?** Yes — but it cannot run first. Producing a
> pushed, tagged version is exactly what Verses 1 and 2 build, so the spike
> sits third by necessity rather than by preference.

This deviates from bit_scope's own spike-ordering rule, which reads:

> **A spike verse** — only building the thing can answer it. This becomes a
> real verse in the delivery order, and because a wrong assumption should fail
> cheap, it goes first.

What was discovered: BIT-41's unknown is about whether a released artifact
propagates to an already-installed consumer, and observing that requires
cutting and pushing a real release upstream. The prework is not scaffolding for
the spike — Verses 1 and 2 are production work that ships, and the 1.1.0 the
spike pushes is kept as this track's real release. The user states that on
previous tracks this was not the case: "there were things we could test without
commiting to."

The user confirmed the shape as authored and labelled it positive feedback. No
change was made to BIT-41.

The consideration absent from the scope skill: it has no test for whether a
spike is itself gated by earlier verses. Its spike guidance covers marking the
verse, phrasing the question so an answer is recognisable, naming what is
downstream, and saying whether the artifact is kept — but it treats the spike's
position as fixed at first, and it asks what depends on the spike without
asking what the spike depends on. The user also named an alternative the skill
does not raise anywhere — splitting into two tracks, the first delivering the
prework and the second opening with the spike — and rejected it on the grounds
that "the value is tightly linked."
